01

Google Business Profile

This one is bigger than everything else on the list put together.

When somebody in Woodstock searches "bathroom remodel near me," Google shows a map with three businesses sitting above the normal results. That map comes from Google Business Profile — not from your website. If you're not in it, you're invisible for the searches that actually bring in work.

Go to google.com/business and claim or open your listing.

Business name

Use exactly what's on your truck and your invoices — nothing more. Google penalises keyword stuffing here, so not "Centerline Construction Bathroom Remodeling Holly Springs."

Business name

Centerline Construction Company, LLC

If your signage just says "Centerline Construction," use that instead — match the real world.

Categories

Your primary category carries more weight than any other single field on the listing.

Primary: General contractor

Secondary:

RemodelerBathroom remodelerDeck builder Siding contractorKitchen remodelerCarpenter Construction company
A judgment call, so you know: "General contractor" is the broadest umbrella and covers additions and commercial work. "Remodeler" is narrower but less contested. Start with General contractor — if you're not showing up for remodel searches in a few months, switch the primary to Remodeler and watch what happens. It's one click and it's reversible.

Address

If you run out of your home and don't have a shop customers visit, hide the address and set it up as a service-area business. Otherwise Google puts your house on the map. You still enter the address — Google needs it to verify you — you just tick the box that hides it.

02

Make sure AI crawlers aren't blocked

Thirty seconds, and it decides whether the llms.txt we just wrote does anything at all.

In Squarespace, look for Settings → Crawlers. Depending on your version it may be under Settings → Marketing → AI Tools, or Developer Tools. There's a switch for blocking AI crawlers, and depending on when your site was set up it may be on by default.

You want it off — crawlers allowed. That's what lets ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI answers read your site and recommend you. If it's blocked, llms.txt does nothing.

Do this one straight after the Google profile. It costs nothing and it quietly undoes a lot of work if it's wrong.

05

Search Console — and Bing

Google Search Console is free, and it tells you things nobody else will: which searches you actually show up for, which pages Google refuses to index, and whether anything's broken. Verify the site at search.google.com/search-console, then submit your sitemap.

Sitemap URL
https://www.centerlineworks.com/sitemap.xml

Bing Webmaster Tools matters far more than its market share suggests, because ChatGPT's web search runs on Bing's index. If Bing hasn't crawled you properly, ChatGPT can't recommend you no matter what llms.txt says. Verify there too and submit the same sitemap.

Fifteen minutes, and almost no local contractor bothers.
06

Clean up the URLs

Two things to check under Settings → Advanced → URL Mappings.

Your FAQ page sits at /faqs-3, which means there was once a /faqs and a /faqs-2. Rename the slug to /faqs and add a redirect so nothing breaks. And if /newservices was ever live publicly, redirect that too.

URL mappings
/faqs-3 -> /faqs 301 /newservices -> /services 301

The 301 tells Google the page permanently moved, so whatever credit the old address built up transfers across instead of being thrown away.

Never delete a page without adding a redirect. That's how sites lose their rankings overnight.
07

Photo filenames and alt text

Squarespace puts the filename straight into the image's web address, and Google reads it. IMG_2657.jpg says nothing about anything. Rename before you upload:

Filename pattern
bathroom-remodel-holly-springs-ga.jpg finished-basement-canton-ga.jpg custom-cabana-woodstock-ga.jpg deck-railing-ball-ground-ga.jpg

Then add alt text describing the actual photo — that's what it's for, and it's also what a blind visitor's screen reader reads out loud. Photos of you should name you in full: "Alfred Tudela, founder of Centerline Construction, on a bathroom renovation in Holly Springs, GA."

09

Keep the reviews coming

43 five-star reviews is a real asset. Two things worth knowing about it.

Google reviews are the ones that rank you. 29 of your 43 are on Google, and that number feeds directly into the map pack. Facebook and Yelp reviews build trust with people who already found you, but they do almost nothing for Google.

Recency counts too. A listing with 29 reviews where the newest is two years old looks worse than one with 15 where the newest is last week.

Get your Google review short link from the Business Profile dashboard — the "Ask for reviews" button — then text it the day you finish, while they're still standing in the new room and still happy.

Review request text

Hey [name] — really enjoyed working on your [bathroom]. If you've got 30 seconds, a quick Google review helps us more than anything else. [link] — thanks! Alfred

Asking on the day you finish roughly triples the response rate compared with asking a week later. Never offer anything in exchange for a review — Google removes them and can suspend the listing.

Straight talk

What's not worth your time

So you don't get sold any of it:

  • Meta keywords tags. Dead since 2009. Google ignores them completely.
  • Buying backlinks or "SEO packages" from anyone who cold-calls or emails you. At best it's wasted money; at worst it gets you penalised.
  • Blogging three times a week. For a local contractor, one genuinely useful page beats thirty thin ones. Your photos and your reviews are worth far more than word count.
  • Chasing llms.txt. We wrote it, it's free, it might help. But it isn't why AI tools will recommend you — the structured data, the FAQ answers and your Google profile are.
  • Anything promising "#1 on Google, guaranteed." Nobody can guarantee that. Anyone who says they can is lying to you.

The unglamorous truth: Google Business Profile plus a steady stream of recent reviews is most of local SEO. Everything else here is finishing work around those two.
