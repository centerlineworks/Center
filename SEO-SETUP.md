# Getting Centerline to the top — the settings checklist

Written for Alfred. Work down the list in order — it's sorted by how much each one actually
moves the needle, not by how hard it is. Everything you need to paste is written out below.

The website itself is already doing its job: structured data on every page, FAQ answers in

## 1. Google Business Profile — do this one first

This is bigger than everything else on the list put together. When somebody in Woodstock
searches "bathroom remodel near me," Google shows a map with three businesses above the normal
results. That map pack comes from Google Business Profile, **not** from your website. If
you're not in it, you're invisible for the searches that actually bring in work.

Go to **google.com/business** and either claim or open your listing.

### Business name
Use exactly what's on your truck and your invoices — nothing more. Google penalises keyword
stuffing here, so **not** "Centerline Construction Bathroom Remodeling Holly Springs."

> Centerline Construction Company, LLC

(If your signage just says "Centerline Construction," use that instead — match the real world.)

### Categories
Primary category carries the most weight of any single field.

- **Primary:** General contractor
- **Secondary:** Remodeler · Bathroom remodeler · Deck builder · Siding contractor ·
  Kitchen remodeler · Carpenter · Construction company

*Judgment call worth knowing about:* "General contractor" is the broadest umbrella and covers
additions and commercial. "Remodeler" is narrower but less contested. I'd start with General
contractor. If after a few months you're not showing up for remodel searches, switch the
primary to Remodeler and watch what happens — it's a one-click change and it's reversible.

### Address
If you run out of your home and don't have a shop customers visit, **hide the address** and set
it up as a service-area business. Google will otherwise put your house on the map. You still
enter the address (Google needs it to verify you), you just tick the box that hides it.

### Service area
Holly Springs · Canton · Woodstock · Ball Ground · Acworth · Kennesaw · Marietta · Roswell ·
Alpharetta · Cherokee County

Don't list twenty towns you'd never drive to — Google waters down your relevance for the ones
that matter. This list is already about as wide as it should go.

### Hours

Set holiday hours when they come up. A listing marked "open" when you're not gets complaints,
and Google notices.

## 2. Check that AI crawlers aren't blocked

Directly connected to the llms.txt we just wrote — and it would be a shame to write that file
and then block the robots that read it.

In Squarespace look for **Settings → Crawlers** (some versions: **Settings → Marketing → AI
Tools**, or **Developer Tools**). There's a switch for blocking AI crawlers, and depending on
when your site was set up it may be **on by default**.

You want it **OFF** (crawlers allowed) — that's what lets ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and
Google's AI answers see your site and recommend you. If any of it is blocked, llms.txt does
nothing.

Takes thirty seconds. Do it right after the Google Business Profile.

## 5. Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

**Google Search Console** (search.google.com/search-console) is free and it tells you the
things nobody else will: which searches you actually show up for, which pages Google refuses to
index, and whether anything is broken. Verify the site, then submit
`https://www.centerlineworks.com/sitemap.xml`.

**Bing Webmaster Tools** matters more than its market share suggests — **ChatGPT's web search
runs on Bing's index.** If Bing hasn't crawled you properly, ChatGPT can't recommend you no
matter what llms.txt says. Verify there too and submit the same sitemap. It's fifteen minutes
and almost no local contractor bothers.

## 6. Clean up the URLs

Two things to check under **Settings → Advanced → URL Mappings**:

- Your FAQ page is at `/faqs-3`, which means there were once a `/faqs` and a `/faqs-2`. Rename
  the page's slug to `/faqs` and add a redirect so nothing breaks:
  ```
  /faqs-3 -> /faqs 301
  ```
- If `/newservices` ever went live publicly, redirect it too:
  ```
  /newservices -> /services 301
  ```

The `301` tells Google the page permanently moved, so whatever credit the old URL built up
transfers to the new one instead of being thrown away. **Never delete a page without adding a
redirect** — that's how sites lose rankings overnight.

## 7. Photo filenames and alt text

Squarespace puts the filename into the image's web address, and Google reads it.
`IMG_2657.jpg` says nothing. Rename before uploading:

```
bathroom-remodel-holly-springs-ga.jpg
finished-basement-canton-ga.jpg
custom-cabana-woodstock-ga.jpg
deck-railing-ball-ground-ga.jpg
```

Then add alt text describing the actual photo — that's what it's for, and it's also what a
blind visitor's screen reader reads out. Photos of you should name you fully: *"Alfred Tudela,
founder of Centerline Construction, on a bathroom renovation in Holly Springs, GA."*

## 9. Keep the reviews coming

43 five-star reviews is a real asset. Two things to know:

**Google reviews are the ones that rank you.** 29 of your 43 are on Google, and that number is
a direct ranking factor for the map pack. Facebook and Yelp reviews are good for trust but do
almost nothing for Google.

**Recency counts.** A listing with 29 reviews where the newest is two years old looks worse
than one with 15 where the newest is last week.

Get your Google review short link from the Business Profile dashboard ("Ask for reviews"), then
text it the day you finish a job, while they're still happy and standing in the new room:

> Hey [name] — really enjoyed working on your [bathroom]. If you've got 30 seconds, a quick
> Google review helps us more than anything else. [link] — thanks! Alfred

Asking on the day you finish roughly triples the response rate versus asking a week later.
Never offer anything in exchange for a review — Google removes them and can suspend the
listing.

**When we cross a milestone**, tell me and I'll update the numbers everywhere at once — the
site's review counter is config-driven, so it's a one-line change that updates the whole page,
plus llms.txt.

## What's not worth your time

Being straight with you, so you don't get sold any of it:

- **Meta keywords tags.** Dead since 2009. Google ignores them completely.
- **Buying backlinks or "SEO packages"** from anyone who cold-calls or emails you. At best it's
  wasted money, at worst it gets you penalised.
- **Blogging three times a week.** For a local contractor, one genuinely useful page beats
  thirty thin ones. Your photos and reviews are worth far more than word count.
- **Chasing llms.txt.** We wrote it, it's free, it might help. But it's not why AI tools will
  recommend you — the structured data, the FAQ answers and your Google profile are.
- **Anything promising "#1 on Google, guaranteed."** Nobody can guarantee that. Anyone saying
  they can is lying.

The unglamorous truth: **Google Business Profile plus a steady stream of recent reviews is
most of local SEO.** Everything else on this list is finishing work around those two.
